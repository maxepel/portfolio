A park, before
it was paper.

The original goal was a pixel-art game. Anyone can build with AI now, but not everyone makes it look good, and plenty of projects stop at V1. Here is how this park got its look.

Finding the feeling.

I wanted the nostalgia of Disneyland without recreating every detail. I made these concepts with GPT Image, working from real park maps, and it took a few tries to polish them. In the game, the light moves from morning to night as you get closer to the end, so I made one for each.

The first playable version paired an illustrated park map with a pixel guest and dialogue panels.

A little person in a big park.

Before the paper characters came pixel travelers. I used these to test different art styles and see how Claude handled them. None of them made it into the game.

The hard part was making one character look right from every side. One side was easy, but not all of them at once. I tried more retro styles as well.

Same feeling. Different texture.

I was always aiming for a pixel-art game, and I expected AI to find it easy, since it is just coordinates and numbers, however it could never replicate an asset pixel for pixel. Neither Claude, Codex, or Gemini could make pixel avatars reliably.

So V2 used open-source avatars from the Universal LPC Character Generator. I wanted the whole process to be hands-off, so I wasn’t going to draw anything myself. They looked fine, but they weren’t my first choice.

Even when making landscapes, the generators took pixel art and turned it to paint. No AI model at the time could hold the correct style across an entire game.

So I asked Claude what it was really capable of, and to my surprise it told me 3D assets. Experimentation began and led me to what is now the fully playable Version 3. While still not perfect, it made enjoyable assets that fit the vibes I was aiming for.

A world that belongs together.

Claude and Codex were better at 3D models than 2D, which surprised me. I would have guessed the opposite. 3D stuck because of what AI could do, not because I planned it. The first models looked bad, but they got better with refinement.

I had Claude and Codex make a few mockups, and none of them felt like Disney. The bright, paper-like style came closest. It is a custom style, made by combining a few games into one.

This game is hard to compare with other AI-built games you may have seen, because I stuck to Claude and Codex only. Many games out there use dedicated asset generators, such as Meshy, that I didn’t have access to. Within that limit, I’m happy with how far it got. Some parts of the game feel completely in tune with the art, and a few are still catching up. With access to those other tools, I think my next game could come close to some of what’s in the industry today.

Thanks for checking out my page!
